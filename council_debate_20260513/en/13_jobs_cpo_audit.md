# SCAILED WP4 Pathfinder — Jobs (CPO) Product & Business Audit

**Audit Date**: 2026-05-14

---

## 1. Apple Setup Assistant Metaphor

This is shit.

Lu Zhao lives in QA/QC culture. Academic medical informatics. EU grant compliance. He wants traceability, not magic. "Setup Assistant" screams black box — the exact thing your email claims you're not. Worse, it suggests a regulatory tool has achieved consumer-grade triviality.

Replace with: "A regulatory compass with guardrails." Or "interactive logic map." Seduce him with structure, audit trails, explainability. Don't seduce with flash. Seduce with certainty.

---

## 2. M1-M2 Three Low-Fidelity Prototypes

Three versions in two months? You just told Epidata you don't know what you're building.

In product, option paralysis kills contracts. Three prototypes says "pick your favorite" — but they hired you to know. This isn't a brand pitch. You're a contractor, not an agency.

Fix: One prototype. Progressive fidelity. M1 = paper wireframes. M2 = clickable after one feedback round. Show confidence. Show the north star. If you need three versions to find your product, do it internally. Never make the client pay for your confusion.

---

## 3. SHAIPED "Reuse vs Rewrite" Wording

Do you want to lose?

You just told Lu Zhao — implicitly — that parts of his previous project are garbage. Even if true, courtship is not surgery time. You're auditioning to be his partner, not his code reviewer.

Burn that sentence. Replace with: "Pathfinder aligns architecturally with SHAIPED principles, extending its foundation for SCAILED-specific compliance workflows." Only discuss gaps after you've earned trust and he's asked your opinion. **Never criticize the client's ex on the first date.**

---

## 4. V2 as Separate Contract — Drawing Boundaries Too Early

Boundaries are good. Legal talk in a courtship email is death.

In a proposal, saying "V2 excluded, separate contract" reads like pulling out a prenup at a romantic dinner. It signals you're already planning the divorce.

Same legal protection, human phrasing: "Phase 1 is ruthlessly scoped to ensure M20 delivery. Future phases will be co-designed after core value is proven." The contract appendix can have the legal exclusion. The email builds trust. Early trust > early protection. Remember: they don't yet know they can trust you.

---

## 5. Rework Billed at Daily Rate

This is adversarial. And it admits your scope is fragile.

You're basically saying: "When you change your mind, the clock starts ticking." But in EU projects, upstream changes are guaranteed. WP3 will deliver garbage. EHDS will change. Either you've priced for it or you haven't.

Better: Absorb 15-20% refinement buffer into the fixed price. Call it "coordinated calibration." If EHDS rewrites laws mid-project — that's a change order. But if Epidata clarifies a requirement? That's the work. Don't nickel-and-dime before you've become partners.

If you must say it: "Refinements within the Schema baseline are included in the fixed price. Structural changes after M3 Schema freeze require mutual agreement."

---

## 6. What the Entire Proposal Most Lacks?

**Soul. The why.**

I read mechanics: Schema, phases, coverage percentages, Docker containers. I did not read about a single living human using this thing.

Missing:
- **Named user stories**. "Dr. Schmidt needs to know within 90 seconds whether her dataset qualifies for secondary use under EHDS Article 34." Where is she? What's her anxiety? What's her relief?
- **Demo**. Where are the screenshots? A 60-second GIF? You can't sell software without showing it. Even if it's faked in Figma.
- **Risk mitigation**, not just risk lists. WP3 unstructured PDF = high risk. Fine. Where's your PLAN B? Manual transcription? Small LLM layer? You can't just list death and turn the page.
- **"Test Drive" definition**. What does it mean? Who tests? With what data? For how long? Vague milestones = missed milestones.
- **Accessibility**. EU project. Legal requirement. Not mentioned.
- **GDPR/DPO touchpoint**. No PII ≠ no compliance theater. Need one line saying DPO reviewed the architecture.
- **Named PM on DataWego side**. Currently faceless.
- **"One more thing."** Every proposal needs one moment of unexpected value. A feature they didn't ask for that makes them smile. Where is it?

Add one line at the end:
